## Questions

**Q1** (2 min) (a) State one observation from the photoelectric effect that the wave model of light could not explain, and how Einstein's photon model accounts for it. (b) Find the energy of one photon of light with wavelength 600 nm.

**Q2** (3 min) Two mirrors make an angle of 100° with each other. A ray is incident on mirror M1 at an angle of 60° to the normal. Find the angle the ray makes with the normal to M2 after it is reflected from both mirrors.

**Q3** (3 min) Light with a vacuum wavelength of 520 nm enters a liquid whose index of refraction is 1.47. (a) What is its speed in the liquid? (b) What is its wavelength in the liquid? (c) What is its frequency in the liquid? (d) A coin lies on the bottom of a tank filled 0.60 m deep with this liquid. Seen from directly above, how deep does the coin appear to be?

**Q4** (4 min) The critical angle for total internal reflection at a liquid-air interface is 40.0°. (a) A ray traveling in the liquid meets the interface at 30.0°. What angle does the refracted ray in the air make with the normal? (b) A ray traveling in air meets the interface at 30.0°. What angle does the refracted ray in the liquid make with the normal? (c) What happens to a ray in the liquid that meets the interface at 50.0°?

**Q5** (4 min) A glass prism ($n = 1.60$) sits in air. Its surfaces 1 and 2 meet at 60.0°. A light beam enters surface 1 at angle of incidence $\theta_1$, crosses the prism, and strikes surface 2 at exactly the critical angle (same layout as S1 Q6). Determine $\theta_1$.

**Q6** (2.5 min) (a) Use Snell's law to explain why total internal reflection can occur when light goes from water into air but never from air into water. (b) In the Huygens construction for refraction, ray 2 travels $A'C = v_1\Delta t$ in medium 1 while the wavelet from A grows to $AB = v_2\Delta t$ in medium 2, and both right triangles share the hypotenuse $AC$. Show that this leads to Snell's law.

**Q7** (4 min) Unpolarized light of intensity 80.0 W/m² passes through three polarizers. The first has a vertical transmission axis, the second is rotated 35.0° from the first, and the third is rotated 80.0° from the first. (a) Find the intensity after each polarizer. (b) Find the final intensity if the middle polarizer is removed.

**Q8** (3 min) Unpolarized light in air reflects from the flat surface of a liquid. The reflected light is completely polarized when the angle of incidence is 53.0°. (a) What is the index of refraction of the liquid? (b) What is the angle of refraction? (c) What is the critical angle for this liquid-air interface?

**Q9** (6 min) An equilateral prism (apex angle 60.0°) has $n = 1.64$ for violet light and $n = 1.60$ for red light. White light is incident on it at 55.0°. (a) Find the angular separation between the emerging violet and red rays. (b) At what angle of incidence would violet light pass through at minimum deviation, and what is $\delta_{min}$ for violet?

**Q10** (2 min) (a) A raindrop high in the sky looks red to you, while one lower down looks violet. Explain using the angles at which the colours leave the drop, and state which colour the drop deviates more. (b) A classmate's notes say: "As $\lambda$ increases, $f$ decreases and photon energy increases." Correct the statement.

## Answer key

### Q1
(a) The kinetic energy of the electrons ejected from the metal does not depend on the light's intensity (L1 s.6). In Einstein's model the light's energy arrives in photons of energy $E = hf$, so the energy each electron can get is set by the frequency, not by the brightness (L1 s.7).

(b)
$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.00\times10^8}{600\times10^{-9}} = 5.00\times10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$$
$$E = hf = (6.63\times10^{-34})(5.00\times10^{14}) = 3.32\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$$

### Q2
- Reflection at M1: the ray leaves at 60° to the normal, so at $90^\circ - 60^\circ = 30^\circ$ to M1's surface.
- Triangle formed by the ray and the two mirrors: $30^\circ + 100^\circ + x = 180^\circ$, so $x = 50^\circ$ between the ray and M2's surface.
- Angle with M2's normal: $90^\circ - 50^\circ = 40^\circ$. It reflects at the same 40°.

**Answer: 40°.** Check: turn at M1 $= 180^\circ - 2(60^\circ) = 60^\circ$, at M2 $= 180^\circ - 2(40^\circ) = 100^\circ$, total $160^\circ = 360^\circ - 2(100^\circ)$.

### Q3
(a) $v = \dfrac{c}{n} = \dfrac{3.00\times10^8}{1.47} = 2.04\times10^8$ m/s

(b) $\lambda_n = \dfrac{\lambda_0}{n} = \dfrac{520}{1.47} = 354$ nm

(c) $f = \dfrac{c}{\lambda_0} = \dfrac{3.00\times10^8}{520\times10^{-9}} = 5.77\times10^{14}$ Hz, unchanged by the liquid. Check: $\lambda_n f = (354\times10^{-9})(5.77\times10^{14}) = 2.04\times10^8$ m/s, matching (a).

(d) Object in the liquid ($n_1 = 1.47$), observer in air ($n_2 = 1.00$):
$$d' = d\,\frac{n_2}{n_1} = 0.60 \times \frac{1.00}{1.47} = 0.41\ \text{m}$$

### Q4
Index of the liquid from the critical angle:
$$\sin\theta_c = \frac{1.00}{n} \Rightarrow n = \frac{1}{\sin 40.0^\circ} = \frac{1}{0.6428} = 1.556$$

(a) $30.0^\circ < \theta_c$, so the ray exits: $1.556\sin 30.0^\circ = 1.00\sin\theta$, $\sin\theta = 0.778$, **$\theta = 51.1^\circ$**.

(b) $1.00\sin 30.0^\circ = 1.556\sin\theta$, $\sin\theta = 0.3214$, **$\theta = 18.7^\circ$**.

(c) $50.0^\circ > 40.0^\circ = \theta_c$: **total internal reflection**. There is no refracted ray; the light reflects back into the liquid at 50.0°.

### Q5
- The beam meets surface 2 at the critical angle: $r_2 = \theta_c = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{1}{1.60} = 38.7^\circ$.
- Prism geometry (L3-Disp s.7): $r_1 + r_2 = A$, so $r_1 = 60.0^\circ - 38.7^\circ = 21.3^\circ$.
- Snell at surface 1: $\sin\theta_1 = 1.60\sin 21.3^\circ = 1.60 \times 0.364 = 0.582$.

**Answer: $\theta_1 = 35.6^\circ$.**

### Q6
(a) Snell's law gives $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{n_1}{n_2}\sin\theta_1$. From air into water, $n_1/n_2 = 1/1.33 < 1$, so $\sin\theta_2 < \sin\theta_1 \le 1$ and a refracted ray exists at every angle. From water into air, $n_1/n_2 = 1.33 > 1$, so $\sin\theta_2$ reaches 1 when $\sin\theta_1 = 1/1.33$, that is at $\theta_c = 48.8^\circ$. Beyond that no refraction angle exists and all the light is reflected.

(b) In the two right triangles with hypotenuse $AC$:
$$\sin\theta_1 = \frac{A'C}{AC} = \frac{v_1\Delta t}{AC}, \qquad \sin\theta_2 = \frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{v_2\Delta t}{AC}$$
Dividing, $\dfrac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2} = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2}$. With $v_1 = c/n_1$ and $v_2 = c/n_2$:
$$\frac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2} = \frac{c/n_1}{c/n_2} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} \Rightarrow n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$$

### Q7
(a)

- After polarizer 1 (unpolarized in): $I_1 = \dfrac{80.0}{2} = 40.0$ W/m²
- After polarizer 2 (35.0° from polarizer 1): $I_2 = 40.0\cos^2 35.0^\circ = 40.0 \times 0.671 = 26.8$ W/m²
- After polarizer 3 ($80.0^\circ - 35.0^\circ = 45.0^\circ$ from polarizer 2): $I_3 = 26.8\cos^2 45.0^\circ = 26.8 \times 0.500 = 13.4$ W/m²

(b) Without the middle polarizer, polarizers 1 and 3 are 80.0° apart:
$$I = 40.0\cos^2 80.0^\circ = 40.0 \times 0.0302 = 1.21\ \text{W/m}^2$$
Taking out the middle sheet drops the output from 13.4 to 1.21 W/m².

### Q8
(a) Light comes from air, so $\tan\theta_p = n$: $n = \tan 53.0^\circ = 1.33$.

(b) At the polarizing angle the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular: $\theta_r = 90.0^\circ - 53.0^\circ = 37.0^\circ$. Check with Snell: $\sin 53.0^\circ = 1.327\sin\theta_r$ gives $\sin\theta_r = 0.602$, $\theta_r = 37.0^\circ$.

(c) $\theta_c = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{1.00}{1.327} = 48.9^\circ$.

### Q9
(a) Trace each colour with $\sin r_1 = \dfrac{\sin i_1}{n}$, $r_2 = A - r_1$, $\sin i_2 = n\sin r_2$. Here $\sin 55.0^\circ = 0.8192$.

| Step | Violet, $n = 1.64$ | Red, $n = 1.60$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\sin r_1 = 0.8192/n$ | 0.4995, $r_1 = 29.97^\circ$ | 0.5120, $r_1 = 30.80^\circ$ |
| $r_2 = 60.0^\circ - r_1$ | $30.03^\circ$ | $29.20^\circ$ |
| TIR check, $\theta_c = \sin^{-1}(1/n)$ | $37.6^\circ > r_2$, exits | $38.7^\circ > r_2$, exits |
| $\sin i_2 = n\sin r_2$ | 0.8208, $i_2 = 55.17^\circ$ | 0.7806, $i_2 = 51.32^\circ$ |
| $\delta = i_1 + i_2 - A$ | $50.17^\circ$ | $46.32^\circ$ |

Angular separation $= 55.17^\circ - 51.32^\circ = 3.85^\circ$ (the same as $\delta_V - \delta_R$).

(b) At minimum deviation $r = A/2 = 30.0^\circ$:
$$\sin i = 1.64\sin 30.0^\circ = 0.820 \Rightarrow i = 55.1^\circ$$
$$\delta_{min} = 2i - A = 2(55.08^\circ) - 60.0^\circ = 50.2^\circ$$
So the 55.0° beam in part (a) is almost exactly at violet's minimum deviation, which is why violet's $r_1$ and $r_2$ came out nearly equal.

### Q10
(a) Violet light leaves the drop at 40° to the incoming sunlight and red at 42° (L3-Disp s.17). From a drop high in the sky, the 42° red ray heads down steeply enough to reach your eye, while the 40° violet ray passes over your head, so the drop looks red. A lower drop sends violet to your eye. Violet is deviated **more**: it is turned through $180^\circ - 40^\circ = 140^\circ$, red through $180^\circ - 42^\circ = 138^\circ$ (textbook p. 913; the slide's wording is reversed, see [[Cheat Sheet#Errata|Errata]] item 5).

(b) "As $\lambda$ increases, $f$ decreases and photon energy **decreases**," because $E = hf = hc/\lambda$.
