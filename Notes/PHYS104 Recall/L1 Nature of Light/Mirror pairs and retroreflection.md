# Mirror pairs and retroreflection

**Definition.** When a ray reflects from two mirrors in turn, its final direction follows from the law of reflection at each mirror plus the angle sum of the triangle formed by the ray and the mirrors. Retroreflection is the special case where the ray leaves parallel to its original path, travelling back toward its source (L1 slide 15).

## Explanation
For mirrors meeting at angle φ:
1. At M1, the reflected ray makes $90^\circ - \theta_1$ with M1's surface.
2. In the triangle (ray, M1, M2): $(90^\circ - \theta_1) + \phi + x = 180^\circ$, where x is the angle between the ray and M2's surface.
3. The angle with M2's normal is $90^\circ - x$.

Each reflection turns a ray by $180^\circ - 2\theta$; adding the two turns is a quick check.

> [!quote] Textbook note
> Textbook p. 903: two reflections always turn a ray by $360^\circ - 2\phi$. For $\phi = 90^\circ$ the turn is 180°, whatever the angle of incidence, which is retroreflection.

## Worked detail: L1-Ex1 recomputed
Mirrors at 120°; the ray hits M1 at 65° from the normal (L1 slide 14).
1. At M1: $90^\circ - 65^\circ = 25^\circ$ from the surface.
2. Triangle: $25^\circ + 120^\circ + x = 180^\circ$, so $x = 35^\circ$.
3. From M2's normal: $90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$, matching the slide.

Check: turns of $(180^\circ - 130^\circ) + (180^\circ - 110^\circ) = 120^\circ = 360^\circ - 2(120^\circ)$.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Calculation (L1 slide 14)
> Mirrors meet at 120°, and a ray hits M1 at 70° from its normal. Find the angle it makes with M2's normal.

> [!success]- Answer
> 90° − 70° = 20° from M1's surface; 20° + 120° + x = 180° gives x = 40°; so 90° − 40° = 50° from M2's normal.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Calculation (L1 slide 15)
> By how much do two reflections turn a ray when the mirrors meet at 120°? At 90°?

> [!success]- Answer
> 360° − 2(120°) = 120°. At 90°: 360° − 180° = 180°, so the ray goes back the way it came (retroreflection).
