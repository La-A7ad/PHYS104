# Law of reflection

**Definition.** For light reflecting from a smooth surface, the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, $\theta_1' = \theta_1$, both measured from the normal. The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal lie in one plane (L1 slide 10).

## Explanation
The normal is the line perpendicular to the surface at the point where the ray lands. Measuring from it, not from the surface, is the convention for the whole chapter. Two consequences (slide 11):
- A ray along the normal ($\theta_1 = 0$) reflects straight back on itself.
- Light paths are reversible: a ray sent back along the reflected path retraces the incident path.

Smooth surfaces give **specular** reflection: parallel incoming rays leave parallel (slide 12). Rough surfaces give **diffuse** reflection: the rays leave in many directions, because each tiny patch of the surface has its own normal (slide 13). Both kinds obey $\theta_1' = \theta_1$ at every point.

## Worked detail: surface angle to normal angle
A ray meets a mirror at 72° from its surface.
1. From the normal: $90^\circ - 72^\circ = 18^\circ$, so $\theta_1 = 18^\circ$.
2. Law of reflection: $\theta_1' = 18^\circ$, on the other side of the normal, which is again 72° from the surface.
3. The ray's direction of travel turns by $180^\circ - 2(18^\circ) = 144^\circ$.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Calculation (L1 slide 10)
> A ray meets a mirror at 35° from its surface. Give the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection as optics measures them.

> [!success]- Answer
> Both are 90° − 35° = 55°, measured from the normal.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Comparison (L1 slides 12 to 13)
> Does diffuse reflection break the law of reflection? Explain.

> [!success]- Answer
> No. Each tiny patch of a rough surface obeys θ₁' = θ₁ about its own normal. Those normals point in different directions, so the reflected rays do too.
