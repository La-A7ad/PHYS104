# Ray approximation

**Definition.** In ray (geometric) optics, light in a uniform medium is treated as travelling in straight lines called rays. A ray changes direction only where it meets a different medium, or where the medium's optical properties vary (L1 slide 8).

## Explanation
A ray is drawn perpendicular to the wave fronts and points the way the wave travels (slide 8). Near a point source the wave fronts are spheres and the rays fan out from the source; for a plane wave the fronts are flat and the rays are parallel.

Whether the ray picture survives an opening of width d depends on the wavelength λ (slide 9):

| Case | What the wave does after the opening |
|---|---|
| λ ≪ d | keeps going straight, apart from small edge effects: rays work |
| λ ≈ d | spreads out after the opening (diffraction) |
| λ ≫ d | the opening acts like a point source of spherical waves |

Everything after this in the chapter, from mirrors to prisms, assumes the first case.

## Worked detail
Light of wavelength 500 nm passes through a pinhole 2.0 mm wide:
$$\frac{d}{\lambda} = \frac{2.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}}{5.0\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}} = 4.0\times10^{3}$$
So λ ≪ d, and the light can be drawn as straight rays. Send the same light through a slit 500 nm wide and λ ≈ d: it spreads out instead.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Prediction (L1 slide 9)
> Infrared light with λ = 5 μm passes through a hole 5 μm wide. Straight beam or spreading wave?

> [!success]- Answer
> λ ≈ d, so it spreads out after the hole (diffraction). The ray approximation fails here.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Explanation (L1 slide 8)
> What is the geometric relationship between a ray and the wave fronts it belongs to?

> [!success]- Answer
> The ray is perpendicular to every wave front it crosses and points in the direction the wave travels.
