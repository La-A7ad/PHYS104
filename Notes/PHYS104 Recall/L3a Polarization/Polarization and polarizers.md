# Polarization and polarizers

**Definition.** Light is linearly polarized when its electric field vibrates in one direction only. In unpolarized light the field vibrates in all directions across the beam (L3a slide 7). A polarizer transmits the field component parallel to its transmission axis and absorbs the perpendicular component (slide 9).

## Explanation
The rope picture (slide 8): shake a rope up and down and the wave is polarized vertically. A vertical slot lets it through; a horizontal slot blocks it. A polarizing sheet does the same job for the electric field, with its transmission axis acting as the slot.

Two rules cover almost every problem:
1. **Unpolarized light in**: the transmitted intensity is half the incident intensity, whatever the polarizer's orientation, and the light that comes out is polarized along the axis (slide 9).
2. **Polarized light in**: Malus's law applies, $I = I_0\cos^2\theta$, where θ is the angle between the light's polarization and the axis (slide 11).

![[L3a-s09-polarizer.png]]
*Figure (L3a slide 9): a polarizer halves unpolarized light and polarizes it along its axis.*

A polarizer works by selective absorption. Reflection near the polarizing angle is a second way to polarize light (slides 16 to 17).

## Worked detail: crossed polarizers
Unpolarized light of 10 W/m² meets a vertical polarizer: 5.0 W/m² comes out, polarized vertically. A second polarizer with a horizontal axis is at 90° to that polarization: $5.0\cos^2 90^\circ = 0$, so nothing gets through. Turn the second sheet to 45° and $5.0\cos^2 45^\circ = 2.5$ W/m² gets through.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Prediction (L3a slide 9)
> Unpolarized light of 12 W/m² falls on a polarizer. You slowly rotate the polarizer through a full turn. What does a light meter behind it read?

> [!success]- Answer
> A steady 6.0 W/m². Unpolarized light has no preferred direction, so the orientation does not matter.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Prediction (L3a slide 12)
> Polarized light meets an analyzer whose axis is perpendicular to the light's polarization. What gets through?

> [!success]- Answer
> Nothing: cos² 90° = 0. This is the minimum of Malus's law.
