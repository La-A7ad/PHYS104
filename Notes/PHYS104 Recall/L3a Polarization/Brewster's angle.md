# Brewster's angle

**Definition.** The polarizing angle, or Brewster's angle, $\theta_p$ is the angle of incidence at which reflected light is completely polarized. It satisfies $\tan\theta_p = n_2/n_1$; from air, $\tan\theta_p = n$ (L3a slides 17 to 18).

## Explanation
When unpolarized light reflects from a surface, the reflected and refracted beams are usually both partially polarized (slide 16). At $\theta_p$ the reflected beam is completely polarized, vibrating perpendicular to the plane of incidence, while the refracted beam stays partially polarized. At that angle the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular (slide 17).

The law in three lines:
1. Perpendicular rays: $\theta_r = 90^\circ - \theta_p$.
2. Snell's law: $n_1\sin\theta_p = n_2\sin(90^\circ - \theta_p) = n_2\cos\theta_p$.
3. So $\tan\theta_p = n_2/n_1$.

$n_1$ is the medium the light comes from. For glass under water, use $n_2/n_1 = n_{glass}/n_{water}$, not $n_{glass}$ alone.

## Worked detail: glass with n = 1.50, in air
$\theta_p = \tan^{-1}(1.50) = 56.3^\circ$, and the refracted ray travels at $90^\circ - 56.3^\circ = 33.7^\circ$ from the normal.

Snell check: $\sin 56.3^\circ = 0.832$, and $1.50\sin 33.7^\circ = 0.832$. The two sides agree, so the perpendicular-ray condition and Snell's law hold together.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Calculation (Tutorial 2, Q5 style)
> Light reflected from a glass plate in air is completely polarized at 57.0°. Find n and the angle of refraction.

> [!success]- Answer
> n = tan 57.0° = 1.54; θr = 90.0° − 57.0° = 33.0°.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Explanation (L3a slide 17)
> At the polarizing angle, which beam is completely polarized, and what is the angle between the reflected and refracted rays?

> [!success]- Answer
> The reflected beam; the refracted beam is only partly polarized. The two rays are 90° apart.
