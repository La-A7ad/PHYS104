# Electromagnetic spectrum

**Definition.** The electromagnetic spectrum is the full range of electromagnetic waves ordered by wavelength or frequency: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays (L3a slide 6).

## Explanation
An electromagnetic wave is an electric field and a magnetic field oscillating perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel, so it is a transverse wave. Accelerating charges produce such waves, and in vacuum all of them travel at $c = 3\times10^8$ m/s (slide 5).

Because $c = \lambda f$ is fixed in vacuum, a long wavelength means a low frequency. Photon energy follows frequency ($E = hf$, L1 slide 7). So going from radio toward gamma rays:
- wavelength falls,
- frequency rises,
- photon energy rises.

Visible light is a narrow band, from about 400 nm (violet) to 750 nm (red).

## Worked detail: the edges of the visible band
Using $f = c/\lambda$:
- Red edge, $7.5\times10^{-7}$ m: $f = (3.0\times10^8)/(7.5\times10^{-7}) = 4.0\times10^{14}$ Hz
- Violet edge, $4.0\times10^{-7}$ m: $f = (3.0\times10^8)/(4.0\times10^{-7}) = 7.5\times10^{14}$ Hz

Both match slide 6's labels. The photon energies are $2.65\times10^{-19}$ J (red) and $4.97\times10^{-19}$ J (violet). Slide 6's second arrow line gets this direction wrong; see [[L3a Polarization#Errata|L3a errata]].

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Calculation (L3a slide 6)
> An FM radio wave has a wavelength of 3.0 m. What is its frequency?

> [!success]- Answer
> f = c/λ = (3.0 × 10⁸)/3.0 = 1.0 × 10⁸ Hz, which matches slide 6's 3 m mark.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Comparison (L3a slide 6)
> Which carries more energy per photon, ultraviolet or infrared light, and why?

> [!success]- Answer
> Ultraviolet. It has a shorter wavelength, so a higher frequency, and E = hf.
