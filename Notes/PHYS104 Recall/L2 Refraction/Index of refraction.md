# Index of refraction

**Definition.** The index of refraction of a medium is $n = c/v$: the speed of light in vacuum, $c = 3.00\times10^8$ m/s, divided by the speed of light in the medium. It has no unit (L2 slide 4).

## Explanation
Light is slower in any material than in vacuum, so n > 1 for every medium; the course takes n = 1 for air. A larger n means slower light: $n \propto 1/v$. The **relative index** of medium 2 with respect to medium 1 is ${}_1n_2 = n_2/n_1$ (slide 5).

The index decides which way a ray bends (slide 5):
- Into a higher index ($n_2 > n_1$, so $v_2 < v_1$): toward the normal.
- Into a lower index ($n_2 < n_1$, so $v_2 > v_1$): away from the normal.

The refraction rule on slide 3, $\sin\theta_1/\sin\theta_2 = v_1/v_2$, becomes Snell's law once each speed is written as $c/n$. At a single surface, the angle between the refracted ray and the extended incident ray is the deviation $\beta = \lvert\theta_1 - \theta_2\rvert$ (slide 4).

## Worked detail: how slow is light in diamond?
With n = 2.42, the diamond value in L2-Ex3:
$$v = \frac{c}{n} = \frac{3.00\times10^8}{2.42} = 1.24\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$$
That is about 41% of its speed in vacuum.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Calculation (L2 slide 4)
> Light travels at 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s in a liquid. Find the liquid's index of refraction.

> [!success]- Answer
> n = c/v = (3.00 × 10⁸)/(2.25 × 10⁸) = 1.33.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Comparison (L2 slide 5)
> Find the relative index of glass (n = 1.50) with respect to water (n = 1.33). Does light going from water into glass bend toward or away from the normal?

> [!success]- Answer
> ₁n₂ = 1.50/1.33 = 1.13. It is greater than 1, so the light enters a higher index and bends toward the normal.
