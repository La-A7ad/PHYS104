# Wavelength in a medium

**Definition.** When light passes into a medium of index n, its frequency stays the same while its speed and wavelength both shrink by the factor n: $v = c/n$ and $\lambda_m = \lambda_{air}/n$ (L2 slide 8).

## Explanation
For any wave $v = \lambda f$ (slide 6): λ is the distance between successive crests, f the number of complete cycles per second. Across a boundary f is fixed, so $v_1 = \lambda_1 f$ and $v_2 = \lambda_2 f$, which gives
$$\frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{v_2}{v_1} = \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}$$
A higher index means a shorter wavelength. The product $n\lambda$ is the same in every medium, and equals the vacuum wavelength.

> [!quote] Textbook note
> Textbook p. 907: if the frequency changed at the boundary, crests would arrive at one rate and leave at another, and waves would pile up or vanish there. Nothing at the boundary can do that, so f is the same on both sides.

## Worked detail: inside L2-Ex2's slab
L2-Ex2 gives $n = \sin 40^\circ/\sin 26^\circ = 1.466$ for light of wavelength 550 nm in air. Inside the slab:
- $\lambda = 550/1.466 = 375$ nm
- $v = (3.00\times10^8)/1.466 = 2.05\times10^8$ m/s
- $f = c/\lambda_{air} = (3.00\times10^8)/(550\times10^{-9}) = 5.45\times10^{14}$ Hz, the same inside and outside

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Calculation (Tutorial 1, Q2 style)
> Light with a vacuum wavelength of 500 nm enters a plastic with n = 1.60. Find its wavelength and speed in the plastic.

> [!success]- Answer
> λ = 500/1.60 = 313 nm; v = (3.00 × 10⁸)/1.60 = 1.88 × 10⁸ m/s.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Explanation (L2 slide 8)
> Which of v, λ and f is the same in air and in glass, and why?

> [!success]- Answer
> f. The number of crests reaching the boundary each second must equal the number leaving it, so the frequency set by the source carries through, while v and λ both drop by the factor n.
