# Rainbow

**Definition.** A rainbow is sunlight dispersed by raindrops. Light refracts into each drop, reflects from its back surface and refracts out again, leaving at 40° (violet) to 42° (red) from the incoming sunlight (L3b slide 17).

## Explanation
The colours separate at the first refraction, with violet bent most. After one reflection at the back of the drop and a second refraction on the way out, violet leaves at 40° to the incoming sunlight and red at 42°.

Measure angles from the **antisolar direction**: the direction pointing straight away from the Sun, toward the shadow of your head. The ray that reaches your eye from a drop runs along your line of sight, so the drop's 40° or 42° becomes the angle between your line of sight and the antisolar direction:
- drops 42° from the antisolar point send you red;
- drops 40° from it send you violet.

So the bow is a pair of circles around the antisolar point, red outside (radius 42°) and violet inside (40°). In turning angle, violet is deviated more (140°) than red (138°).

## Worked detail: how high is the bow?
The antisolar point lies as far below the horizon as the Sun is above it. With the Sun 10° up, the top of the red arc is 42° − 10° = 32° above the horizon, and the top of the violet arc 40° − 10° = 30°.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Prediction (L3b slide 17)
> Which colour lies on the outer edge of the primary bow?

> [!success]- Answer
> Red, because it leaves the drops at the larger angle, 42°, to the sunlight.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Calculation (L3b slide 17)
> The Sun is 30° above the horizon. How high is the top of the red arc?

> [!success]- Answer
> 42° − 30° = 12° above the horizon.
