# Dispersion

**Definition.** Dispersion is the dependence of a medium's index of refraction, and so of the wave speed in it, on the wavelength of the light: $n = n(\lambda)$ (L3b slide 3).

## Explanation
In vacuum every colour travels at c. In a material the speed $v = c/n(\lambda)$ differs from colour to colour. For most transparent materials over the visible range, n falls as λ rises (slide 4):
$$n_{violet} > n_{blue} > n_{green} > n_{yellow} > n_{red}$$

![[L3b-s03-n-vs-lambda.png]]
*Figure (L3b slide 3): n against vacuum wavelength for six materials.*

By Snell's law, colours with different n refract by different angles. In a prism this gives the chain on slide 5: $\lambda_V < \lambda_R$, so $n_V > n_R$, so $v_V < v_R$, so violet bends more and $\delta_V > \delta_R$.

**Monochromatic** light has a single wavelength (a laser, a sodium lamp); a prism deviates it but cannot spread it. **Polychromatic** light mixes many wavelengths (sunlight, an incandescent bulb); a prism fans it into a spectrum (slide 2).

Colour bands by vacuum wavelength (slide 4): violet 380 to 450 nm, blue 450 to 495 nm, green 495 to 570 nm, yellow 570 to 590 nm, orange 590 to 620 nm, red 620 to 750 nm.

## Worked detail: speeds in BK7 glass
With the indices from L3b-Ex2:
- Violet: $v = (3.00\times10^8)/1.5224 = 1.971\times10^8$ m/s
- Red: $v = (3.00\times10^8)/1.5143 = 1.981\times10^8$ m/s

Violet is slower by only about 0.5%, yet that is enough to separate the colours.

## Recall
> [!question] Recall 1 · Comparison (L3b slide 4)
> In glass, which travels faster, green light or blue light?

> [!success]- Answer
> Green: $n_{green} < n_{blue}$, so $v = c/n$ is larger for green.

> [!question] Recall 2 · Calculation (L3b slide 3)
> Find the speed of red light in a flint glass with $n_R = 1.62$.

> [!success]- Answer
> v = (3.00 × 10⁸)/1.62 = 1.85 × 10⁸ m/s.
